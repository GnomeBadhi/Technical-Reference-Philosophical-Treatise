Plain‑Language Summary (With PDE Introduction)

This project explains a simple idea with big consequences:

> When a system becomes more coherent, it reorganizes itself, and that reorganization pushes energy outward.

To make that idea precise, we build a mathematical structure called a kernel, and then — when the system has space and time — we describe how coherence and energy move using something called a PDE.

Let’s break it down.

---

1. What “coherence” means

Every system has three different “views” or “aspects.”  
If those three aspects agree with each other, the system is coherent.  
If they disagree, the system is disordered.

We measure disagreement, flip it upside down, and call the result coherence.

- High coherence → everything lines up  
- Low coherence → everything is fighting itself  

This gives us a single number between 0 and 1 that tells us how aligned the system is.

---

2. Energy splits into two parts

Every system has a fixed amount of total energy, but it can be stored in two ways:

- Internal energy (held inside the system)  
- Radiated energy (energy that has already been released outward)

As coherence increases:

- Internal energy goes down  
- Radiated energy goes up

So coherence doesn’t “create” energy — it moves energy from inside the system to the outside.

---

3. Radiation only happens after a threshold

The system doesn’t radiate energy all the time.

It only radiates when coherence passes a certain threshold.

Below the threshold:  
- the system is stable  
- no energy is released  

Above the threshold:  
- the system becomes “too coherent”  
- excess energy is pushed outward  

This gives a clean rule for when radiation starts.

---

4. What a PDE is (plain language)

A PDE — a partial differential equation — is a tool for describing how something changes across space and time at the same time.

You use a PDE when:

- the system isn’t just a single point  
- but spread out across a region  
- and different parts of the system influence each other  

Examples of things described by PDEs:

- heat spreading through metal  
- waves moving across water  
- pressure changes in the atmosphere  
- electrical signals moving through a material  

A PDE is the mathematical way to say:

> “This thing changes here because of what’s happening over there.”

---

5. Why PDEs matter for the kernel model

If coherence is happening in a whole region — not just at one point — then coherence becomes a field:

- a value at every location  
- that changes over time  
- and spreads or smooths out  
- and pushes energy outward when it gets too high  

A PDE lets us describe:

- how coherence spreads  
- how coherence increases or decreases  
- how radiated energy moves outward  
- how the two influence each other  

In other words:

> The PDE is the engine that turns the kernel model into a dynamic system.

It lets us simulate or analyze how coherence and energy evolve in real space and real time.

---

6. Putting it all together

The full picture looks like this:

1. Coherence measures how aligned the system is.  
2. Energy splits into internal and radiated parts.  
3. Radiation happens only when coherence crosses a threshold.  
4. PDEs describe how coherence and energy move through space and time.  

This gives you a complete, mathematically grounded way to model:

- how coherence forms  
- how it spreads  
- how it reorganizes energy  
- and how that energy gets released  

All from first principles.
